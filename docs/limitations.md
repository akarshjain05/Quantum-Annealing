# Limitations

Documented directly and prominently, on the belief that this increases credibility rather than reducing it.

## Data
- All data is synthetic, generated by `backend/app/seed/seed_data.py` from a fixed random seed. It is designed to be *realistic in shape* (different volumes, volatilities, and timing per corridor) but is not derived from any real bank's actual flows.
- 90 days of history is a thin window for volatility estimation; real deployments would want multiple years, including at least one full stress period.

## Forecasting
- The forecasting module (`forecasting/forecast.py`) blends a 14-day moving average and an EWMA - deliberately simple and explainable, not a production-grade time-series model. Spec-listed alternatives (ARIMA, gradient boosting, Prophet-style decomposition) are not implemented.
- Demand is assumed approximately normal for the shortfall-probability calculation; real payment demand is often skewed or fat-tailed, especially around holidays.

## Risk model
- `P(shortfall | L) = Phi((mu - L) / sigma)` is explicitly an illustrative model (documented as such everywhere it appears), not a validated actuarial or historically-backtested risk model.
- Loss-given-shortfall figures are randomly seeded per corridor within a plausible range, not derived from any real operational loss data.

## Optimization
- Liquidity is discretized into 8 fixed buckets. The true continuous optimum may sit between buckets - finer discretization trades solution precision for a larger QUBO and longer solve time (currently well within demo-scale performance, ~100-300ms for 88 variables).
- When the global capital cap is disabled, the QUBO is exactly block-diagonal across corridors. When enabled, the capital cap constraint introduces real cross-corridor coupling via a slack-variable encoding (see `docs/qubo-mathematics.md` §3). This cross-coupling can create complex energy landscapes that challenge the simple coordinate-descent refinement pass, requiring the solver to use iterated local search (reheating) to escape local minima.
- Simulated annealing with a penalty-based one-hot encoding has a documented pathology (the two-hot energy barrier, §6 of the math doc) that we hit, diagnosed, and fixed during this build's own testing - flagged prominently rather than swept under the rug.

## Quantum computing
- **No quantum hardware is used anywhere in this system.** The solver is classical simulated annealing, full stop. "Quantum-ready" refers only to the fact that the QUBO matrix this code builds is in the correct input format for a quantum annealer - it is not a claim of quantum execution, quantum advantage, or quantum performance.

## Agent
- Intent detection is keyword-phrase scoring, not an LLM-based classifier - reliable and testable, but less flexible than free-text understanding. A question phrased unusually may fall through to the general-snapshot fallback rather than the intended scenario.
- Corridor-code extraction from free text is a simple regex - works for "USD_INR" / "USD to INR" phrasing, not arbitrary natural language references to a corridor.
- The optional LLM-phrasing enhancement hook is present in code but untested (no API key available in the build environment) - see `docs/agent-architecture.md`.

## Infrastructure
- No live banking rail integration, nostro connectivity, SWIFT messaging, or payment execution of any kind exists or is claimed.
- Redis/Celery are wired into `docker-compose.yml` for future async job scaling but are not required or used by the current synchronous, demo-scale optimization path.
- `docker compose up --build` was written and reviewed but not executed end-to-end in the environment this was built in (no Docker daemon available there) - verify it yourself before relying on it for a live demo.

## Scope vs. the full hackathon specification
This build implements a deliberately-scoped subset of a much larger specification (30+ tables, 15 pages, full Postgres/Redis/Docker execution, live LLM agent, exhaustive test coverage across every module). See the README's "Deliberate simplifications" table for the full list of what was cut and why - none of it touches the core optimization math, which is fully real and tested.

## Regulatory
- Every item in the REGULATION knowledge corpus is a synthetic placeholder, explicitly labeled as such, and the agent is hard-coded to say it cannot verify any of them as real regulatory requirements. See `docs/sandbox-readiness.md`.
